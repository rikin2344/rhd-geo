⚡Load Ratings

Dynamic Load (Cr) 2.86kN
Static Load (Cor) 1.08kN
Load Capacity 292kg force

💡What This Means

Dynamic Load (2.86kN = 292kg): Maximum force while rotating. Exceeding this causes rapid wear and failure within hours.
Static Load (1.08kN = 110kg): Maximum force when stationary. Use 80% for safety (88kg max recommended).
Calculate Your Load: Measure shaft forces, belt tensions, or gear loads. Include shock/vibration factors.
Failure Warning: Overloading causes bearing raceways to crack and balls to flatten permanently.

🔄Speed Limits

Grease Lubrication 28,000 RPM
Oil Lubrication 36,000 RPM
Recommended Max 25,200 RPM (90%)

💡What This Means

Grease Limit (28,000 RPM): Heat generation increases exponentially above this. Grease breaks down, causing seizure.
Oil Advantage (36,000 RPM): Better heat dissipation allows 29% higher speed. Required for high-speed spindles.
Safe Operating Speed: Use 90% of limit (25,200 RPM) for maximum L10 life of 10,000+ hours.
Failure Mode: Exceeding speed causes cage failure, ball skidding, and catastrophic overheating within minutes.

How do I know if the 607 bearing is the right choice for my 7mm shaft application?

Perfect for 7mm shafts requiring 2.86kN load capacity. Check shaft diameter, housing space (19mm), and load requirements match specifications.

The 607 is ideal for compact precision applications where space is limited but performance is critical. Wrong bearing selection leads to premature failure, equipment damage, and costly downtime. The 607's compact design makes it perfect for small motors, precision instruments, and miniature mechanical systems.
How To Handle It
Verify: 1) Shaft is exactly 7mm diameter, 2) Housing accommodates 19mm outer diameter, 3) You have 6mm width clearance, 4) Loads stay under 2.86kN dynamic/1.08kN static. For compact precision instruments and small motors, the 607 provides optimal balance of size and performance.
The 607 is perfect for applications where every millimeter counts - compact but mighty performance.

What makes the 607 different from the 608 - should I use the larger bearing instead?

The 607 (7x19x6mm) is more compact than 608 (8x22x7mm) but has slightly lower load capacity (2.86kN vs 3.32kN). Choose based on space and load requirements.

The 607 proves that good things come in small packages - maximum performance in minimal space.

How do I determine if I need sealed (2RS) or shielded (ZZ) 607 bearings for my application?

Use 607-2RS for dusty/wet environments (complete sealing) and 607-ZZ for clean environments needing dust protection. Open 607 for highest speed applications.
Why This Matters
Seal selection directly impacts bearing life and performance. Wrong seal choice leads to contamination (if under-protected) or excessive friction and heat (if over-sealed). The 607's small size makes it particularly sensitive to contamination and seal drag.
How To Handle It
Choose based on environment: 607-2RS for wet/dusty conditions (19,600 RPM max), 607-ZZ for general protection (25,200 RPM max), Open 607 for clean, high-speed applications (28,000 RPM max). Consider maintenance access - sealed bearings are maintenance-free but non-serviceable.
Match seal type to environment - over-sealing reduces speed, under-sealing reduces life.
🔨 Installation & Maintenance ▼

What's the proper way to install a 607 bearing without damaging it during assembly?

Use proper bearing pullers and presses - never hammer install. Apply force to the tight-fit ring only. Small bearings like 607 are particularly sensitive to installation damage.
Why This Matters
The 607's small size makes it vulnerable to installation damage. Improper installation can cause internal damage, misalignment, or premature failure. Hammer installation can cause ball indentations, raceway damage, or cage deformation that may not be immediately visible but drastically reduce bearing life.
How To Handle It
Use appropriate tools: bearing press or puller set for 7mm bore. Apply force evenly to the tight-fit ring (inner ring for shaft mounting, outer ring for housing mounting). Never apply force through rolling elements. Use proper shaft tolerance (h6) and housing tolerance (H7) for optimal fit.
Proper installation tools are essential for 607 bearings - the small size offers no forgiveness for installation errors.

How often should I re-lubricate my 607 bearings, and what grease should I use?

Re-grease every 6-12 months or 3000-6000 operating hours. Use high-quality lithium complex grease (NLGI Grade 2) suitable for small bearings.
Why This Matters
Small bearings like the 607 have limited grease capacity, making lubrication quality critical. Poor or degraded lubrication leads to increased friction, overheating, and rapid wear. The 607's high-speed capability (28,000 RPM) demands quality lubrication for optimal performance.
How To Handle It
Use lithium complex grease (NLGI Grade 2) for temperatures -30°C to +120°C. For high-speed applications (25,000+ RPM), use synthetic grease. Apply minimal quantity - over-greasing causes overheating. Monitor temperature during operation - excessive heat indicates lubrication problems.
Less is more with small bearings - proper grease type matters more than quantity.

My 607 bearing is running hot - what are the most common causes and solutions?

Common causes: excessive speed (over 28,000 RPM), overloading (above 2.86kN), over-greasing, tight fits, or misalignment. Check load, speed, and installation first.
Why This Matters
Heat generation in small bearings like the 607 indicates internal stress or lubrication problems. Excessive heat rapidly degrades bearing materials and lubrication, leading to catastrophic failure. Small bearings have limited heat dissipation capacity, making temperature control critical.
How To Handle It
Systematic check: 1) Verify speed under 28,000 RPM, 2) Confirm load under 2.86kN, 3) Check shaft/housing tolerances (h6/H7), 4) Verify proper lubrication (not over-greased), 5) Check alignment. Operating temperature should stay under 70°C. Address root cause before continuing operation.
Small bearings show problems quickly - high temperature is an early warning requiring immediate attention.

What's the expected service life of a 607 bearing in typical small motor applications?

In properly designed applications with correct installation and lubrication, expect 10,000-50,000 hours of service life, depending on load and speed conditions.
Why This Matters
Service life expectations help with maintenance planning and total cost calculations. The 607's small size means precision manufacturing and installation are crucial for achieving rated life. Proper application engineering can extend life significantly, while poor practices can reduce life by 80% or more.
How To Handle It
Maximize life through: proper installation (no hammer), quality lubrication (lithium complex grease), staying within ratings (2.86kN, 28,000 RPM), maintaining proper tolerances, and protecting from contamination. Monitor performance - unusual noise or vibration indicates developing problems.
Quality 607 bearings properly applied can outlast the equipment they're installed in.
🔍 Troubleshooting & Problem Solving ▼

My 607 bearing is making noise - how do I diagnose the problem and determine if it's dangerous?

Light humming is normal. Grinding indicates contamination/wear, squealing suggests lubrication failure, knocking means severe damage. Stop operation if noise is loud or irregular.
Why This Matters
Bearing noise is an early warning system, especially critical in small bearings like the 607 used in precision applications. Different sounds indicate different failure modes, and early intervention can prevent catastrophic failure that could damage expensive precision equipment.
How To Handle It
Diagnose by sound: Normal = light humming, Contamination = grinding/rough noise, Lubrication failure = squealing/chirping, Severe damage = knocking/irregular noise. For grinding: clean and re-lubricate. For squealing: check lubrication. For knocking: replace immediately. Use stethoscope or vibration analysis for precise diagnosis.
In precision applications, any unusual noise from a 607 bearing warrants immediate investigation.

The 607 bearing in my precision instrument is causing accuracy problems - what could be wrong?

Likely causes: worn clearances, contamination, improper preload, or thermal expansion. Small changes in 607 bearing performance significantly affect precision instruments.
Why This Matters
Precision instruments rely on exact bearing performance for accuracy. The 607's small size means even minor wear or contamination can cause measurable performance degradation. Internal clearance changes, runout, or vibration directly affect instrument precision and repeatability.
How To Handle It
Systematic diagnosis: 1) Check runout with dial indicator, 2) Measure internal clearance, 3) Verify lubrication condition, 4) Check for contamination, 5) Confirm proper preload/clearance class. For precision work, consider C2 (tight) clearance bearings and high-grade tolerances (ABEC-7 or better).
Precision applications demand precision bearings - invest in higher grade 607 bearings for critical instruments.

Why do my 607 bearings fail prematurely in small motor applications, and how can I prevent it?

Common causes: installation damage, contamination, overloading, or excessive speed. Small motors often have harsh operating conditions that require careful bearing selection and protection.
Why This Matters
Small motors with 607 bearings often operate continuously in challenging conditions. Premature failure leads to motor replacement, downtime, and customer complaints. The 607's compact size offers limited tolerance for abuse, making proper application engineering essential for reliable operation.
How To Handle It
Prevention strategies: Use sealed bearings (607-2RS) for protection, ensure proper motor design (adequate shaft support, proper tolerances), avoid overloading (stay under 2.86kN), protect from contamination, use quality grease, and design for thermal expansion. Consider higher-grade bearings for critical applications.
Motor bearing failure is often a system problem, not just a bearing problem - address the root cause.

How do I know when to replace a 607 bearing versus trying to repair or re-lubricate it?

Replace if there's any roughness, play, or damage to races/balls. Small bearings like 607 aren't economically repairable - replacement is usually the only option.
Why This Matters
The 607's small size and precision construction make field repair impractical and uneconomical. Attempting to repair damaged bearings often leads to worse problems. Early replacement prevents collateral damage to expensive equipment and maintains system reliability and precision.
How To Handle It
Replace when: 1) Any roughness when turning by hand, 2) Visible wear on races or balls, 3) Excessive play or runout, 4) Contamination that can't be cleaned, 5) Overheating episodes. Only clean and re-lubricate if bearing feels smooth and has proper clearances. For critical applications, replace at first sign of degradation.
With 607 bearings, replacement costs far less than the consequences of failure - replace early rather than risk equipment damage.
